# Japan's public health insurance and the medical fee schedule (English explainer)

*Synthetic explainer written for the UW Lens demo corpus. Not an official MHLW publication and not insurance or medical advice.*

## Universal coverage
Everyone living in Japan must join public health insurance: employee health insurance (kenko hoken) through an employer, National Health Insurance (kokumin kenko hoken, NHI) run by municipalities for the self-employed and others, or the Late-Stage Elderly Medical Care System for people aged 75 and over.

## Co-payment rates
Patients pay a share of costs at the counter: generally 30% for ages 6 to 69, 20% for children before primary school and for ages 70–74, and 10% for ages 75 and over (20% or 30% for those with higher incomes). The High-Cost Medical Expense Benefit (kogaku ryoyohi) caps monthly out-of-pocket spending.

## The fee schedule (shinryo hoshu)
Prices for medical services and drugs are set nationally in a points-based fee schedule, with one point equal to 10 yen. The schedule is revised every two years. Because prices are uniform, claims data (receipts, called "rezept") are standardized and coded, which makes them useful evidence when reviewing treatment history.

## Health checkups
Employers must offer annual health checkups, and insurers provide Specific Health Checkups (tokutei kenshin) for ages 40–74 focused on metabolic syndrome. Applicants for private insurance in Japan are often asked to submit recent checkup results instead of undergoing a separate paramedical exam.
