# Asthma — clinical reference summary

*Synthetic educational summary in the style of public-domain NIH/NHLBI topic pages, written for the UW Lens demo corpus. Not medical advice and not an official NIH publication.*

## What it is
Asthma is a chronic lung disease in which the airways become inflamed and narrow, causing wheezing, chest tightness, shortness of breath and coughing, often at night or early morning. Triggers include allergens, respiratory infections, exercise, cold air and smoke.

## Assessing control
Control is judged by daytime symptoms, night-time awakenings, use of quick-relief (rescue) inhalers, limits on normal activity, and lung function measured by spirometry (FEV1). Severe exacerbations needing oral corticosteroids, emergency visits or hospitalization — especially intensive care or intubation — signal higher risk.

## Treatment
Inhaled corticosteroids are the main long-term controller medicine, often combined with a long-acting bronchodilator. Biologic medicines are used for some severe asthma. An asthma action plan helps people respond early to worsening symptoms.
