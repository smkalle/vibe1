# Atrial Fibrillation — clinical reference summary

*Synthetic educational summary in the style of public-domain NIH/NHLBI topic pages, written for the UW Lens demo corpus. Not medical advice and not an official NIH publication.*

## What it is
Atrial fibrillation (AFib) is an irregular and often rapid heart rhythm that starts in the upper chambers of the heart. It can come and go (paroxysmal), last longer than 7 days (persistent), or be permanent. Some people feel palpitations, shortness of breath or tiredness; others have no symptoms.

## Stroke risk
Blood can pool in the atria and form clots, raising the risk of stroke about five-fold. Doctors estimate stroke risk with the CHA2DS2-VASc score, which adds points for congestive heart failure, hypertension, age 75 or older (2 points), diabetes, prior stroke or TIA (2 points), vascular disease, age 65–74, and female sex. Anticoagulants (blood thinners) such as apixaban or warfarin are recommended for many people with higher scores.

## Treatment
Rate control (beta blockers, calcium channel blockers), rhythm control (antiarrhythmic drugs, cardioversion) and catheter ablation are options. Treating sleep apnea, high blood pressure, obesity and heavy alcohol use reduces AFib episodes.
