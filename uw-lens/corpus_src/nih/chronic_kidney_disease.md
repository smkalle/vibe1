# Chronic Kidney Disease — clinical reference summary

*Synthetic educational summary in the style of public-domain NIH/NIDDK topic pages, written for the UW Lens demo corpus. Not medical advice and not an official NIH publication.*

## What it is
Chronic kidney disease (CKD) means the kidneys are damaged and cannot filter blood as well as they should for three months or longer. Diabetes and high blood pressure are the most common causes.

## Staging
Kidney function is estimated from a blood creatinine test as the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR, mL/min/1.73 m²):
- G1: 90 or above with signs of damage
- G2: 60–89
- G3a: 45–59
- G3b: 30–44
- G4: 15–29
- G5: below 15 (kidney failure)

Damage is also measured by albumin in the urine (urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, UACR): A1 below 30 mg/g, A2 30–300 mg/g, A3 above 300 mg/g. Risk rises with both lower eGFR and higher albuminuria.

## Treatment
Controlling blood pressure and blood glucose slows CKD. ACE inhibitors or ARBs and SGLT2 inhibitors protect the kidneys in many people. Advanced disease may need dialysis or a kidney transplant.
