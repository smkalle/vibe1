# High Blood Pressure (Hypertension) — clinical reference summary

*Synthetic educational summary in the style of public-domain NIH/MedlinePlus topic pages, written for the UW Lens demo corpus. Not medical advice and not an official NIH publication.*

## What it is
Blood pressure is the force of blood pushing against artery walls, written as systolic over diastolic (for example 120/80 mmHg). High blood pressure often has no symptoms, which is why it is called a "silent" risk factor for heart disease, stroke and kidney disease.

## Categories (adults, office readings)
- Normal: systolic below 120 and diastolic below 80.
- Elevated: systolic 120–129 and diastolic below 80.
- Stage 1 hypertension: systolic 130–139 or diastolic 80–89.
- Stage 2 hypertension: systolic 140 or higher or diastolic 90 or higher.
- Hypertensive crisis: above 180/120 — needs urgent care.

Diagnosis relies on the average of two or more readings on two or more occasions. Home or 24-hour ambulatory monitoring helps rule out "white-coat" hypertension.

## Treatment
Lifestyle changes include reducing salt, the DASH eating pattern, weight loss, regular exercise, limiting alcohol and quitting smoking. Common medicines are thiazide diuretics, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs) and calcium channel blockers. Many people need two or more medicines to reach their target.

## Target organ damage
Long-term high pressure can cause left ventricular hypertrophy (a thickened heart muscle seen on ECG or echocardiogram), heart failure, chronic kidney disease, retinopathy and stroke. Evidence of organ damage indicates higher risk than the blood pressure number alone.
